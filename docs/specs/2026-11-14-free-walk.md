# Free walk: first-person walking with hitboxes (behind `features.freeWalk`)

Date: 14 Nov 2026 · Owner: Asilbek (controls, hitboxes, UI/UX) · Scene files touched: tell Shoxjahon aka.

## Goal

The player walks the base in first person, on top of what already works: the six standing points, the tap-to-float
markers and the phase floats all stay. Team decision of 14 Nov evening (docs/STATUS.md): the game gets walking and
collisions. This is the small, dependency-free version of that: no physics engine, no character model. It does not
replace `features.character` (astronaut model + Rapier); it is a playable step towards it and a fallback for it.

## How to try it

`?walk` in the address turns it on for one visit (any build, also the live link); `features.freeWalk = true` turns it
on for everyone. `?walk&debug`, or F3 while walking, shows the hitboxes and a readout (position, speed, frame rate).

## Behaviour

| What | Rule |
|---|---|
| Move | W/S or ↑/↓ ahead and back, A/D step sideways, ←/→ turn (so the keyboard alone is enough). Touch: a stick on the left, shown on touch screens only. |
| Look | Unchanged: drag on the scene (yaw free, pitch ±35°, turn ≤ 60°/s). No pointer lock: taps on buildings and the DOM buttons keep working. |
| Keys | Read by `KeyboardEvent.code`, so W A S D work on a Russian or Uzbek layout. All keys are let go on `blur` and when the tab is hidden. Arrows do not scroll the page. Ignored while typing and with Ctrl/Alt/Cmd. |
| Speed | 2.0 m/s; full speed in about 0.15 s, a stop in about 0.1 s; a diagonal is no faster. Fixed 60 Hz tick, at most 0.1 s owed after a stalled frame. |
| Direction | Relative to the camera's yaw only; looking up or down changes nothing. |
| Eye | 1.7 m above the ground under the walker (`groundHeight`), no bob, no roll, no shake. |
| Collision | The walker is a disc of radius 0.35 m on the ground plane. Solids are discs and bars from `src/scene/layout.ts`, each 0.1 m bigger than what you see. A step is cut into pieces of at most half the body, then pushed straight out of whatever it overlaps: it stops, slides along, never tunnels, never sticks. |
| Edge | 60 m from the middle of the base: an invisible wall you slide along. |
| Inside | Tapping the door floats you in, as before. Inside you walk on a floor of radius 1.25 m. Tap a marker to go back out. |
| Floats | A float (a marker tap, a new phase) always wins over the keys and stops the walker. The marker of the point you walked away from shows again. |
| Off | No walking on the start and end screens, during the launch and during the truth sequence. |

Hitboxes: habitat, garden, workshop, lander, Scout, power hub, dish (discs) and the solar wing (a bar 0.5 m thick).

## Files

- `src/walk/collide.ts` disc-against-discs-and-bars collision · `move.ts` speed and heading · `input.ts` keys and
  stick · `walker.ts` the fixed-tick walker · `world.ts` this base's hitboxes · `enabled.ts` the switch.
  Pure TypeScript apart from `enabled.ts`; allocation-free per frame.
- `src/scene/Rig.tsx`: once a float has arrived, the walker moves the standing spot (about 30 changed lines).
- `src/scene/WalkDebug.tsx`: the F3 view. `src/scene/Stage.tsx`: mounts it when free walk is on.
- `src/ui/play/WalkStick.tsx` and `src/ui/Hud.tsx`: the touch stick; walking only while the game is on screen.
- Tests: `src/walk/*.test.ts` (23): walls, corners, tunnelling at 5 frames a second, sliding, the edge, every
  standing point free and reachable on foot, each hitbox from 12 sides, Alt-Tab, frame-rate independence.

With the flag off the game behaves exactly as before.

## Not in this step

- Carrying (battery pick up / place with E, drop with Q), the wrist tablet key, 1/2/3 on choices.
- Pointer-lock mouse look, the goal arrow at the visor edge, a controls card, a pause screen.
- Moving hitboxes (Scout driving away), wall rings as solids, a slope limit (the playable ground is gentle).
- Not yet tried on a real phone; the stick's place next to the plan tablet needs a look at 360×640.

## Origin

Written for this repo from the team's handoff note "first-person walk" (sections 4–6) with Claude Code. The open
source game ASTRA (another Space Apps team, MIT) was studied as a reference for how walking can feel; none of its
code or assets is copied here (`add-asset`: no competitors' code).
